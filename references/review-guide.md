# Review guide

Show the artifact itself at every gate. A contact sheet or short preview helps, but it should link to the editable or full-resolution result. Ask for the decision only after the artifact is reviewable. If the user supplies corrections, apply them and present the updated version at that gate.

| Gate | What the reviewer should be able to judge |
|---|---|
| 1 Script | Opening makes the topic clear quickly; main claim fits the source; language and length suit the audience; ending is intentional. |
| 2 Storyboard | Every narration beat has a visual purpose; progression is understandable without decorative filler; labels and transitions are specific enough to produce. |
| 3 Audio | Voice is intelligible and correctly pronounced; tone and speed fit; every approved line is present; no obvious clipping or unwanted silence. |
| 4 Timeline | Scene starts and endings match speech; captions appear at the right words; gaps and transitions feel intentional. |
| 5 Style | Palette/type/motion work together, suit the audience and topic, and are feasible with available tools; key content has room around platform controls. |
| 6 Static frames | First three scenes establish the visual system; text is readable at phone size; important labels do not fight captions or fall outside the safe area. |
| 7 First animation | Motion explains or supports the narration; captions, audio, and graphics stay synchronized; scene joins look deliberate. |
| 8 Full animation | Remaining scenes use the approved system consistently; key changes of concept are visible; no stray lines, hidden text, or awkward cuts remain. |
| 9 Final video | The complete cut communicates the intended message and is technically playable. Approval applies to the exact delivered file. |

For technical QA, record output resolution, aspect ratio, fps, codecs, duration, audio presence and level, caption count/text consistency, full-file decode, and a few representative frames from each scene. Check the target platform preview when accessible. If it is unavailable, say which platform behavior has not been verified; do not call an internal safe-area overlay a platform certification.
